DECK: Set 1 — Q17–42 · immunity, Hb, haemostasis, fluids

Q: The complement effect is activated by:
NO: 17
A. T-helper cell
B. Antigen antibody complex
C. C2
D. C3-C4
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Antigen–antibody complexes trigger the classical complement pathway.

Q: IgG:
NO: 18
A. Responsible for primary immunity
B. Activate mast cells and basophiles
C. Responsible for mucosal immunity
D. Help in neonate immunity
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: IgG crosses the placenta, giving passive neonatal immunity. (IgM = primary response, IgE = mast cells/basophils, IgA = mucosal.)

Q: Regarding Hb, which of the following is correct:
NO: 19
A. Fetal Hb has less affinity to O2 than HbA
B. In adults Hb A is formed by 2a 2 epsilon chains
C. Gower 1 Hb is one of the embryological Hb
D. Hb S results from deletion in a chain
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Embryonic Hbs are Gower 1, Gower 2 and Portland. HbF has HIGHER O2 affinity; HbA = 2α2β; HbS is a point substitution (β6 Glu→Val), not a deletion.

Q: In normal healthy person, which of the following cells have the shortest life span:
NO: 20
A. Macrophages
B. Memory T cells
C. Neutrophils
D. Monocyte
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Neutrophils survive only ~6–10 h in blood (a few days in tissue).

Q: Regarding erythropoietin hormone:
NO: 21
A. Is polypeptide
B. absent hypoxia would have little role in erythropoiesis
C. Mainly produced in liver
D. Its deficiency leads to hypochromic microcytic anemia
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: EPO release is driven by hypoxia. It is mainly renal (not liver) and its deficiency gives normocytic normochromic anaemia.
NOTE: Option A is arguable — EPO is a glycoprotein hormone. B is the unambiguous choice.

Q: Which one of the following is increased by parasitic infection:
NO: 22
A. Eosinophil
B. Neutrophil
C. B lymphocyte
D. Tlymphocyte
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Parasites and allergy → eosinophilia.

Q: Which one of the following is true about specific immunity:
NO: 23
A. Very rapid and more stronger effect
B. Changed with repeated infection by the same pathogen
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Specific (adaptive) immunity has memory, so the response changes/strengthens on re-exposure.
NOTE: Source options B and C were blank; shown here as A and B.

Q: MHC 2 Proteins:
NO: 24
A. Coupled with intracellular antigens
B. Recognised by CD8 (Cytotoxic T cells)
C. Recognised by CD4 (helper T cells)
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: MHC II presents extracellular antigen to CD4 helper T cells; MHC I presents intracellular antigen to CD8 cells.
NOTE: Source option C was blank; remaining options shown as A–C.

Q: Immunoglobulin that is found in body secretions is:
NO: 25
A. Ig E
B. Ig A
C. Ig G
D. Ig M
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Secretory IgA (dimer with J chain + secretory piece).

Q: Which of the following would best explain prolonged bleeding time (BT)?
NO: 26
A. Hemophilia A
B. Hemophilia B
C. Thrombocytopenia
D. Warfarin use
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: BT tests platelet plug/vascular phase. Haemophilia and warfarin affect the coagulation cascade (prolong CT/PT/aPTT, not BT).

Q: CD4 is a marker of:
NO: 27
A. B cells
B. Helper T cells
C. Cytotoxic cells
D. An activated macrophage
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: CD4 = helper T cells (the HIV target); CD8 = cytotoxic T cells.

Q: Normal Red blood cell:
NO: 28
A. Rupture when suspended in 0.35% sodium chloride solution
B. Are fragile than spherocyte
C. Count 5 million in female
D. Volume is 85 femtoliter
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Source key: A (complete haemolysis at ~0.35% NaCl). Spherocytes are MORE fragile than normal cells.
NOTE: D (85 fL) is also within the normal MCV range — check against your key.

Q: The cell that does endocytosis and presents antigens is:
NO: 29
A. Natural killer cell
B. Macrophages
C. Neutrophils
D. Eosinophils
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Macrophages phagocytose and present antigen via MHC II.

Q: Bleeding from small cut in the skin:
NO: 30
A. Stops within 6 minutes in normal people
B. Is prolonged in severe factor viii deficiency
C. Is greater from cold skin than from warm skin
D. Requires activation of fibrinogen by thrombin
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Normal bleeding time ≈ 2–6 min. It is NORMAL in haemophilia; bleeding is greater from warm (vasodilated) skin.

Q: Generalized edema caused by:
NO: 32
A. Failure of left ventricle
B. Central venous pressure
C. Pulmonary artery stenosis
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Source key B. Raised central venous pressure (right-sided failure) causes generalized edema; left failure gives pulmonary oedema.
NOTE: Source wording for B is incomplete — presumably 'increased' central venous pressure.

Q: What are the following combination used to measure intracellular fluid volume?
NO: 33
A. Radioactive sodium & heavy water
B. Tritium oxide & Evan’s blue dye
C. Sucrose & Evan’s blue dye
D. Deuterium oxide & iodine labeled albumin
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: ICF can’t be measured directly: ICF = total body water − ECF. Heavy water gives TBW; radioactive sodium gives ECF.

Q: The globin part of haemoglobin F consists of
NO: 34
A. 2 alpha and 2 delta chains
B. 2 beta and 2 alpha chains
C. 2 gamma and 2 alpha chains
D. 2 gamma and 2 delta chains
E. 2 beta and 2 gamma chains
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: HbF = α2γ2.

Q: The following are natural anticoagulants except:
NO: 35
A. thromboxane A2
B. prostacycline
C. protein C
D. antithrombin III
E. plasminogen
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Thromboxane A2 is pro-aggregatory/pro-clotting (platelet-derived).
NOTE: Plasminogen is a fibrinolytic proenzyme, not a classic anticoagulant, but TXA2 is the clear key.

Q: Concerning vitamin B12:
NO: 36
A. is a fat soluble vitamin
B. is found in green vegetable
C. is absorbed in the duodenum
D. antagonized folic acid
E. is required for myelin formation
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: B12 is water-soluble, from animal foods, absorbed in the terminal ileum with intrinsic factor, and needed for myelin.

Q: Physiological jaundice:
NO: 37
A. is due to rhesus incompatibility
B. appears in the first 24 hours after birth
C. is treated by exchange transfusion
D. is due to excessive RBC break down
E. is less common in pre term babies
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Breakdown of fetal RBCs + immature hepatic conjugation. Appears after ~24–48 h, is MORE common in preterms.

Q: Vitamin K deficiency leads to decrease production of the following factor:
NO: 38
A. I
B. VII
C. VIII
D. IX
E. XII
ANSWER: B, D
EXPLANATION: Vitamin K-dependent factors: II, VII, IX, X (plus proteins C and S).
NOTE: Both VII and IX are correct here.

Q: In a 30 years old man red marrow will be found at all these sites except:
NO: 39
A. skull
B. ribs
C. pelvic bones
D. shaft of the femur
E. sternum
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: In adults, red marrow persists in flat bones/axial skeleton and proximal ends of long bones; shafts are fatty (yellow) marrow.

Q: Reticulocyte count in normal blood is
NO: 40
A. not present
B. 1%
C. 3%
D. 5%
E. 10%
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Normal ≈ 0.5–2%.

Q: In obstructive jaundice:
NO: 41
A. bilirubin is excreted in urine
B. stool colour is dark
C. urobilinogen in urine is increased
D. reticulocyte count is increased
E. van den berg test is indirect
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Conjugated (water-soluble) bilirubin is filtered → dark urine; stools pale; urobilinogen absent; Van den Bergh is direct.

Q: Which of the following is not an action of thrombin:
NO: 42
A. activation of factor XIII
B. stimulation of tissue plasminogen activator
C. activation of factor X
D. activation of protein C
E. action of factor VIII
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Thrombin activates XIII, V, VIII, XI, protein C (with thrombomodulin) and stimulates tPA release — but not factor X.
