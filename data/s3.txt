DECK: Set 3 — Q43–51 · Hb F, jaundice, thrombin

Q: The globin part of haemoglobin F consists of:
NO: 43
A. 2α and 2β chains
B. 2α and 2γ chains
C. 2α and 2δ chains
D. 2β and 2γ chains
E. 2β and 2δ chains
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: HbF = α2γ2.

Q: The following are natural anticoagulants except:
NO: 44
A. thromboxane A2
B. prostacycline
C. protein C
D. antithrombin III
E. plasminogen
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: TXA2 promotes platelet aggregation and vasoconstriction.

Q: Concerning vitamin B12:
NO: 45
A. is a fat soluble vitamin
B. is found in green vegetables
C. is absorbed in the duodenum
D. antagonized folic acid
E. is required for myelin formation
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: B12 is needed for myelin/neurological function.

Q: Physiological jaundice:
NO: 46
A. is due to rhesus incompatibility
B. appears in the first 24 hours after birth
C. is treated by exchange transfusion
D. is due to excessive RBC break down
E. is less common in pre term babies
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Fetal RBC breakdown + immature liver conjugation.

Q: Vitamin K deficiency leads to decrease production of the following factor:
NO: 47
A. II
B. VII
C. VIII
D. IX
E. XII
ANSWER: A, B, D
EXPLANATION: II, VII, IX (and X) are vitamin K-dependent.
NOTE: II, VII and IX are all correct.

Q: In a 30 years old man red marrow will be found at all these sites except:
NO: 48
A. skull
B. ribs
C. pelvic bones
D. shaft of the femur
E. sternum
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Femoral shaft has yellow (fatty) marrow in adults.

Q: Reticulocyte count in normal blood is:
NO: 49
A. not present
B. 1%
C. 3%
D. 5%
E. 10%
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: ≈ 0.5–2% of RBCs.

Q: In obstructive jaundice:
NO: 50
A. bilirubin is excreted in urine
B. stool colour is dark
C. urobilinogen in urine is increased
D. reticulocyte count is increased
E. van den berg test is indirect
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and appears in urine.

Q: Which of the following is not an action of thrombin:
NO: 51
A. activation of factor XIII
B. stimulation of tissue plasminogen activator
C. activation of factor X
D. activation of protein C
E. activation of factor VIII
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Factor X is activated by the tenase complexes (IXa–VIIIa or VIIa–TF), not by thrombin.
