DECK: Set 2 — Unnumbered mixed questions

Q: Red blood cell formation in children occurs in the:
NO: 1
A. yolk sac
B. adrenal medulla
C. liver and spleen
D. red bone marrow
E. yellow bone marrow
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: After birth, erythropoiesis is in red bone marrow (yolk sac → liver/spleen → marrow in fetal life).

Q: Iron deficiency anemia is characterized by:
NO: 2
A. high PCV
B. folic acid deficiency
C. microcytic hypochromic RBCs
D. macrocytic hypochromic RBCs
E. macrocytic normochromic RBCs
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Low MCV and MCH: microcytic hypochromic.

Q: A person who is blood group O positive, can receive blood from:
NO: 3
A. Blood group A negative persons
B. Blood group A positive persons
C. Blood group AB positive persons
D. Blood group O negative persons
E. Blood group B negative persons
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: O+ has anti-A and anti-B, so he can receive only group O (O− or O+).

Q: B- Lymphocyte:
NO: 4
A. its cytoplasm contains large number of granules
B. is increased in bacterial infections
C. can produce antibodies
D. can phagocytose bacteria
E. its nucleus is kidney shaped
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: B cells differentiate into plasma cells that secrete antibodies.

Q: Platelets:
NO: 5
A. are non-nucleated biconcave cells
B. their count is higher than red blood cell count
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Platelets are anucleate cell fragments; their count (150–400k/µL) is far below RBC count.
NOTE: Source question was cut off after option b. Strictly, platelets are discoid; RBCs are the biconcave ones — A is correct by elimination.

Q: Megaloblastic anaemia occurs in deficiency of:
NO: 6
A. Protein
B. Folic acid
C. Vitamin C
D. Iron
E. Copper
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Folate (and B12) deficiency impairs DNA synthesis.

Q: Iron:
NO: 7
A. Is mainly stored in the form of haemosiderin
B. Deficiency is frequently accompanied by folic acid deficiency
C. Requirements is greater in males
D. Absorption is reduced by achlorohydria
E. Is mainly stored in the spleen
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Gastric acid reduces Fe³⁺ to the absorbable Fe²⁺, so achlorohydria reduces absorption. Requirements are higher in women; stores are in liver/marrow.
NOTE: A is a common trap: the main storage form is ferritin (haemosiderin is the insoluble overflow form).

Q: Phagocytosis is the major function of:
NO: 8
A. B lymphocytes
B. Neutrophils
C. Endothelial cells
D. Basophils
E. Mast cells
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Neutrophils (and macrophages) are the professional phagocytes.

Q: The major function of T lymphocytes:
NO: 9
A. Release of antibodies
B. Killing of parasites
C. Secretion of heparin
D. Phagocytosis of antigen-antibody complexes
E. Secretion of cytokines
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: T cells orchestrate/execute cell-mediated immunity chiefly via cytokines.

Q: B lymphocytes:
NO: 10
A. Cannot form memory cells
B. Mature in lymphoid tissue of the gastrointestinal tract
C. Attacked by HIV
D. Form plasma cells
E. Stimulate macrophages
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Activated B cells differentiate into plasma cells (and memory cells). HIV attacks CD4 T cells.

Q: Which of the following is an antigen presenting cell:
NO: 11
A. Neutrophil
B. Basophil
C. Eosinophil
D. Macrophage
E. T-lymphocyte
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Macrophages, dendritic cells and B cells are APCs.

Q: The following is not a functions of plasma proteins:
NO: 12
A. Blood coagulation
B. Transport of oxygen
C. Transport of iron
D. Development of colloid osmotic pressure
E. Transport of hormones
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: O2 is carried by haemoglobin, not plasma proteins.

Q: A person with blood group AB Rh positive:
NO: 13
A. Has both antibody A and B in plasma
B. Can donate blood to a person with blood group O Rh positive
C. Can receive blood from a person with blood group A Rh negative
D. Has no agglutinogens on red blood cells
E. Is considered as a universal donor
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: AB+ is the universal recipient: no anti-A/B/D antibodies, so A− blood is compatible.

Q: The intrinsic pathway:
NO: 14
A. Takes a shorter time than the extrinsic pathway
B. In vivo can be accelerated by factor VII
C. In vitro does not require factor XII
D. Does not occur in absence of factor VI
E. Requires vitamin K
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Intrinsic pathway uses IX, X, II (vitamin K-dependent). It is slower than the extrinsic pathway and starts with XII in vitro.

Q: The following are functions of thrombin EXCEPT:
NO: 15
A. Breakdown of fibrinogen
B. Activation of factor V
C. Activation of factor VIII
D. Activation of Plasminogen
E. Activation of factor XII
ANSWER: D, E
EXPLANATION: Thrombin cleaves fibrinogen and activates V, VIII, XI, XIII, protein C and platelets. It doesn’t activate factor XII (contact activation) and doesn’t directly activate plasminogen (it only stimulates tPA release).
NOTE: Both D and E are technically 'except' — E is the more classic key.

Q: Concerning the immunoglobulins:
NO: 16
A. IgM can cross the placenta
B. IgA is found in saliva
C. IgD is increases in primary immune response
D. IgE is the most abundant
E. IgG is increased in allergy
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Secretory IgA is present in saliva, tears, milk. IgG is the most abundant and crosses the placenta; IgE rises in allergy.

Q: Passage of neutrophils through the capillary wall is called:
NO: 17
A. Chemotaxis
B. Opsonization
C. Diapedesis
D. Margination
E. Degranulation
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Diapedesis (transmigration).

Q: Plasminogen is activated by all of the following EXCEPT:
NO: 18
A. Urokinase
B. Streptokinase
C. Tissue plasminogen activator (tPA)
D. Factor V
E. Thrombin
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Urokinase, streptokinase and tPA activate plasminogen; factor V is a clotting cofactor.
NOTE: Thrombin acts only indirectly (by releasing tPA), but factor V is the clear key.
